# Why the baseline scores 71.32, and a starter-only path toward 90+

Author: Codex. Date: 7 November 2026. Solver reference: `v0.1.0-baseline`. Status: evidenced diagnosis and proposed experiments; no solver change or new improved score is claimed.

Only the 10 MB docs/master/sample bundle, saved sample predictions and the sample-only catalog were used. No TRAIN/LIVE/FINAL data were read or reconciled for this review. A diagnostic program compared the frozen predictions with sample labels, prepared sample source groups, and traced selected documents. The reconciliation pipeline, official scorer and model training were not rerun.

Reproduce the diagnostic with `python3 self_review/tools/analyze_saved_sample.py`. Output: `self_review/2026-11-07_sample_diagnosis.json`. It contains golden-assisted examples for evaluation only, never runtime features. Fixed bank IDs in that diagnostic locate counterexamples; they are not matching rules.

## 1. Direct answer: the hard part was simplified too much

Yes. The implementation often reduces the task to retrieving a plausible invoice, comparing its amount with the bank line, and assigning an exception. The actual task requires establishing the complete payment event: who paid for whom, which documents and credits are included, which subdivision of a payment file the line represents, and what balance event the payment causes.

The flat item index loses document relationships, the group representation is sometimes too coarse, some extracted PDF facts are not used in decisions, and the policy function is called before candidate completeness is established. The resulting matcher can identify an invoice correctly while explaining the transaction incorrectly.

The 71.32 result has concrete implementation causes. It is not a demonstrated ceiling for a static system, and there has not been a controlled sequence of experiments establishing a genuine plateau. The next step is a series of small, measurable corrections to these mechanisms, rather than assuming more training data is the missing ingredient.

## 2. What the score is actually losing

The local official scorer assigns 50 points to allocation F1, 30 to transaction accuracy and 20 to exception macro-F1.

| Component | Current value | Current score contribution | Gap to perfect contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Allocation F1 | 79.3908% | 39.70 / 50 | 10.30 |
| Complete-transaction accuracy | 75.9383% | 22.78 / 30 | 7.22 |
| Exception macro-F1 | 44.2233% | 8.84 / 20 | 11.16 |

The exception component is the largest arithmetic gap, but many exception errors originate in incomplete item sets. Improving labels alone does not recover missing documents. Holding the other two components fixed, even a perfect exception macro-F1 would produce only 82.48; this is an illustrative calculation, not a realizable oracle experiment or an independent upper bound when components change together.

The pair-level diagnostic is more informative than the 99.598% precision headline:

- There are 35,659 required item pairs and 23,630 predicted item pairs.
- 23,535 predictions have the correct item and an amount within official tolerance.
- **12,097 required item keys are absent.** Only 27 common item keys have wrong amounts.
- **Every required item ID exists in the starter item tables.** Missing invoice records cannot explain those 12,097 missing keys, although missing historical events or auxiliary documents can still affect some decisions.

Of 8,499 bank cases, 6,454 are fully correct and 2,045 are wrong. The mutually exclusive failure breakdown is:

| Failure | Transactions |
|---|---:|
| Item-settlement transaction left unresolved | 1,482 |
| Some required items missing, no extra items | 248 |
| Both missing and extra items | 60 |
| Correct complete item set and amounts, wrong exception | 224 |
| Other category/item routing errors combined | 31 |

Thus 1,790 of the 2,045 wrong cases involve unresolved or incomplete/wrong item sets. A strategy centered on adding more category keywords would address a comparatively small part of the remaining problem.

The largest absent-item-key families are disputes (3,802), noisy references (2,791), invoice/credit netting (1,954), multi-invoice text (1,212), and AP confirming (840). Together these account for 10,599 of 12,097 absent keys, or about **87.6%**. These are diagnostic counts, not independent score-gain estimates.

## 3. Confirmed simplifications, with source-level examples

### A. An incomplete payment becomes a false overpayment

The saved output has 239 `OVERPAYMENT` predictions: only 8 have the correct exception key. Of the 231 false predictions, **228 belong to multi-item golden transactions**, and 230 have missing required items. In 215 false cases, the selected item's amount itself is correct.

This is a direct demonstration of the structural error. A valid component of a payment is being mistaken for the entire payment. `Engine.item_cash` attributes the entire same-currency bank amount to one candidate; `reference_match` can fall back to that candidate after a multi-item set fails reconciliation; `policy` then interprets cash above that candidate balance as overpayment.

The correction is a completeness requirement and recovery of the rest of the item set. Merely suppressing `OVERPAYMENT`, changing the label to blank, or tuning an excess-percentage threshold would not reconstruct the transaction.

### B. The payer is assumed to be the invoice customer

Case `BT-2025-0162749` has EUR 121,990.01 of cash. The sample PDF advice `remittance_advices/DE01/RA_20250604_VMWWELLNESSG_780984.pdf` contains all **36** required documents, and its parsed total equals the bank amount. The baseline outputs `UNIDENTIFIED`.

The advice payer is VMW Wellness GmbH (`CDE01000155`). Its first invoice, `RE250102928`, belongs to RRS Schwimmbadbau GmbH & Co. KG (`CDE01000003`). The customer master explicitly makes VMW its `parent_customer_id`, and both have `group_id=GDE010003`.

The parser retrieves the correct complete advice, but `resolve_entry` scopes document lookup to the payer ID and removes the subsidiary invoice. This does not require a learned guess: the starter already contains the relationship. The decision needs distinct payer and underlying debtor roles, with source-backed parent/subsidiary expansion.

### C. A payment run is treated as one indivisible bank debit

All 67 sample `ap_confirming` cases fail. **42** have an exact named run reference in the sample payment files, so the earlier broad explanation that confirming failures require prior-month files was incomplete.

For `BT-2025-0163248`, cash is **−EUR 1,251.81**. Run `CF010025061071` has 173 document rows totaling EUR 723,164.38. Two rows for the beneficiary Electrónica Gutiérrez S.A. share EndToEndId `CF010025061071-0062`, and both have `transfer_amount=1251.81`. Their document amounts are EUR 611.91 and 639.90, exactly the two required allocations.

The current parser groups non-`transfers` runs by run ID and execution date, then compares the entire run amount with this bank line. It has discarded the useful subdivision. A payment file needs multiple supported grouping levels: run, transfer/beneficiary and any relevant maturity grouping. The bank evidence must select the appropriate level. The exact rule for each run type should be checked against the source fields and official policy, rather than replacing one blanket grouping assumption with another.

### D. PDF text is read but its accounting meaning is not implemented

For `BT-2025-0162798`, the bank text says **`RATE 3/3 RE-NR RE240216242`**. The invoice PDF `pdfs/DE01/ARI_RE240216242.pdf` explicitly gives three instalments: EUR 6,435.74 / 6,435.74 / 6,435.76. The bank pays the final EUR 6,435.76.

The baseline emits the correct item and amount with `PARTIAL`. The golden exception is blank, consistent with the official last-instalment policy. The baseline initialized the item at its full EUR 19,307.24 and did not use the plan/final-instalment evidence to interpret the event.

This example is not blocked solely by absent earlier cash history: the current bank text and PDF identify the final plan payment. Similar structured facts are needed for retention releases, advance deductions, service months and replacements. Merely caching text or setting an `instalments=True` flag is not equivalent to implementing those rules.

### E. Credit notes are flattened into unrelated items

Case `BT-2025-0162763` lists seven invoice references and requires eight item rows. The additional item is credit note `ARC-2025-8724194`, printed number `GS250100175`, amount EUR 219.53. It is not explicitly named in the bank narrative. The source credit-note CSV links it to invoice `RE250106232` through `related_document`.

The canonical catalog drops `related_document`, and the fallback subset pool excludes credit notes. A source-provided relationship that can explain the net cash is therefore unavailable to the matcher. Store and use invoice-to-credit relations, preserving positive submitted credit amounts and negative contributions to net cash. Require compatible party, currency, dates and settlement intent; not every related credit is automatically paid in every invoice payment.

### F. One generic text parser confuses deductions with paid amounts

In advice `remittance_advices/ES01/RA_20250609_SUMINISTROSP_358143.txt`, a line contains invoice `FV25-0404327`, **11,302.46**, then **−2,260.49 DAMAGED GOODS**. The applied amount is **9,041.97**, with `SHORT_DISPUTE`.

`text_advice` takes the last numeric amount as the paid amount and applies `abs`, turning the deduction into a paid amount of 2,260.49. The advice's computed cash total becomes wrong and the existing index fails to retrieve that advice for the transaction. The parser still reports success because it found document rows.

The email and PDF layouts have different monetary semantics. Parse explicit gross, signed deduction and net fields for each observed layout, and validate against the advice's stated total. Count/date/currency/total reconciliation should be part of parser acceptance. A zero layout-error count is not evidence of correct extraction.

## 4. What the strategy should become

Keep the static infrastructure. Change the representation and decision sequence:

1. **Preserve source meaning.** Retain payer/debtor relationships, `related_document`, transfer subdivisions and structured PDF clauses. Keep unknown facts explicit.
2. **Construct complete payment candidates.** A candidate is a supported set of invoices/credits and its cash explanation, not just a ranked invoice. Retrieve through parent/group relationships, advice references, transfer IDs, related credits and party-blocked reference variants.
3. **Check candidate completeness and conflicts.** Verify source totals, signed credit netting, dates/currencies and competing proposals. Preserve partially decoded groups for further work instead of silently converting them into a single-invoice excess.
4. **Interpret the event and apply policy.** Distinguish full payment, final instalment, partial, return/re-presentation, discount, dispute and documented excess using evidence. Update the ledger only after this decision.
5. **Score and trace the exact failure stage.** Separate source extraction, candidate coverage, group selection, amount and exception errors. Keep the official sample score and full-transaction checks.

This can be implemented with ordinary indexed tables/dictionaries and explicit relationship records. A graph database, large optimization package or model is not a prerequisite. A learned ranker becomes useful only after the correct candidate set is being generated and the remaining task is actually ambiguous ranking.

The chronological greedy ledger is also a design weakness, but the present diagnostics do not quantify its causal score impact. Do not credit a proposed global solver with a numerical improvement before a controlled sample experiment.

## 5. How to pursue 90+ using the starter

Treat **90+ on this starter as the development acceptance target**, not a prediction of unseen performance. A component profile of allocation F1 95%, transaction accuracy 90%, and exception macro-F1 85% would score 91.5. This illustrates the balance needed; it is not a measured result or a promise.

Proposed sample-only sequence:

| Experiment | Mechanism | Predetermined checks |
|---|---|---|
| 1: use available structured evidence correctly | Correct email deduction semantics, parent-payer scoping and run subdivisions. | The concrete cases above reconcile correctly; advice totals validate; preserve existing successful structured cases; compare official sample components and source-stage failures. |
| 2: recover complete groups | Preserve/use related credits; resolve truncated multi-document groups within source-backed party scope; gate overpayment on completeness. | More complete item sets and higher recall; reduce the 231 false overpayment keys without merely relabeling incomplete payments; protect genuine unidentified lookalikes and true overpayments. |
| 3: implement document events | Parse instalment schedules/final markers, retention/advance/service-month clauses and return links. | Correct final-instalment and policy boundary behavior; improve per-exception F1 with support counts; distinguish unknown history from ignored current evidence. |
| 4: inspect the remaining gap | Classify residual errors into missing evidence, candidate generation, grouping, state and true ambiguity. | Decide whether the next step is a deterministic rule, parser, model task or agent extraction task; no automatic expansion to the full corpus. |

For each experiment, start with the small counterexample and valid controls, then run the complete sample to detect interactions. Keep the dataset, scorer and baseline output fixed. Run one mechanism or explicitly defined group of related mechanisms at a time, record the actual score delta, and retain a reproducible commit. These experiments are proposed, not executed in this review.

Use generic accounting/source relationships rather than bank-ID or customer-specific answer tables. Perturb irrelevant IDs, row order, payer names with maintained links, and unrelated same-amount distractors to check the stated invariants. The sample has already been inspected, so even a later score above 90 is a development result; it does not establish final-phase generalization.

Only after the starter strategy performs adequately should TRAIN be introduced for a defined residual model task and suitable time/group evaluation split. Additional data will not automatically repair the discarded relationship field, wrong grouping level or misread deduction illustrated here.

## 6. What is and is not established

Established: the current baseline misses many complete groups, misattributes multi-item cash as excess, rejects a known parent/subsidiary payment, collapses a confirming subgroup, ignores a final-instalment clause, drops credit-note relations and misparses at least one advice layout. The cited examples use available starter evidence.

Not yet established: the exact score gain from each correction, the maximum score recoverable from starter-only evidence, the impact of greedy conflict resolution, or that any chosen model will improve the residual. A 90+ target is appropriate for the next development cycle, while an achieved score must come from a new measured implementation.
